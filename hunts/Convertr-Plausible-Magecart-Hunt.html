Executive Summary

A Brazilian Magecart group that Sansec has tracked since July 2024 planted a payment-card skimmer in the analytics script of Convertr, a Brazilian ecommerce platform that runs checkout for dozens of fashion brands. The group appended an obfuscated block to the stock tracker served from Convertr's own self-hosted Plausible Community Edition server, so every merchant that loads the tracker served the skimmer on its checkout pages. This is the group's first supply-chain attack: until now it compromised stores one at a time (Magento, OpenCart, WooCommerce).

The skimmer activates only on Convertr checkout pages, reads card number, expiry, CVV, cardholder name and the Brazilian CPF taxpayer number, joins them into a pipe-delimited record ending in a short victim tag, Base64-encodes it and POSTs it to 1x0.is. Sansec detected it on 2026-10-24, saw it updated on 2026-10-26 to match Convertr's payment API field names, and confirmed it removed on 2026-10-27 after Convertr cleaned the script and upgraded Plausible. Browsers can cache the infected script for up to 24 hours, so exposure runs to about 2026-10-28.

Entry point is unconfirmed. Sansec suspects CVE-2026-8467 (unauthenticated remote code execution in a component library exposed at /storybook in Plausible CE 3.0.0 through 3.2.0, fixed in 3.2.1) but states it has not seen Convertr's server logs and cannot confirm it.

Defender priority. The skimmer runs in shoppers' browsers, which your endpoint sensor does not see. The value of this pack is (1) finding your own staff, QA and kiosk machines that touched a poisoned checkout or resolved the exfiltration domains (Q1 to Q3), (2) auditing any self-hosted Plausible CE you run (Q4 to Q7, N1, N2), and (3) the hardening that keeps third-party scripts from reading payment fields. If you are a merchant on the platform, ask the platform operator to confirm the tracker is clean and tell your payment provider.
