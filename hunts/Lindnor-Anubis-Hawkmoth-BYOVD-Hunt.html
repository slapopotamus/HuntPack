Executive Summary

In October 2026 an attacker using the Anubis ransomware, developed by the RaaS group Symantec tracks as Hawkmoth, hit a U.S. organization with a previously undocumented custom backdoor, Backdoor.Lindnor, and an unusually large set of security-disabling tools. Hawkmoth emerged in December 2024 as a rebrand of Sphinx, and runs an affiliate program, so different affiliates may bring different tooling. The encryption payload (iservice.exe) was blocked, and Symantec found no evidence that files were encrypted.

Lindnor sat on the only two Microsoft SQL Servers among the affected machines, disguised as a Chrome component unpacker (healthcontroller64.exe) and as a VMware file (svchelper.exe), and ran as a service. Within roughly an hour the attackers also dropped at least 13 executables built to terminate security products and five publicly known vulnerable drivers, a bring-your-own-vulnerable-driver (BYOVD) approach. Two of the executables, gbbsym.exe and symgooq.exe, pose as Symantec software and take -l to list and -d to list and remove kernel callbacks. For a second way in, the attackers installed AnyDesk, created a local account, added it to a local group and registered AnyDesk under the SafeBoot Network key so it survives Safe Mode with Networking. Symantec could not determine initial access. Lindnor was also seen at two other unrelated organizations with no ransomware activity.

This pack has 15 CQL hunts built on the one atomic-IOC source, the Symantec report, plus behavioral hunts that survive rebuilt binaries. Every indicator ships detect. No hash or domain is corroborated by a second source, and the AnyDesk binary and the five drivers are legitimate signed files, so nothing here is a block candidate yet.

⚠ Defender priority: turn on the Microsoft vulnerable driver blocklist and the matching attack surface reduction rule first, because the BYOVD step is what blinds the sensor. Then hunt Q6 (gbbsym or symgooq with -d), Q10 and Q11 (Safe Mode registration), Q8 (driver loads) and Q5 (Lindnor C2 lookups). Treat a hit on the SQL Servers as an active intrusion. A sensor that goes quiet during a driver load is the signal, not the exception.
