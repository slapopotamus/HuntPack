Executive Summary

Huntress documented (blog published 2026-10-22, observation window 2026-07-14 to 2026-09-22) a Microsoft 365 campaign in which the operator never needed a password. Victims signed in through an adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) relay that passed the real Microsoft login page and MFA prompt through, and the relay kept the post-MFA "proof of login" token. Within minutes (12 minutes on 2026-07-21, four minutes on 2026-09-22) the stolen session reappeared from an address in 193.26.115.0/24, a block owned by the German hosting provider 1337 Services GmbH (AS210558 per Huntress and Krebs on Security). Huntress named the technique "token spraying": the same pattern as password spraying, but with many stolen tokens replayed at volume across many organizations. Tokens also came from device-code phishing and from social engineering during a legitimate login.

Huntress responded to incidents at 79 organizations, recording 497 successful sign-ins against more than 200,000 failed attempts. It saw at least 48 rogue Entra devices registered, existing MFA methods wiped and replaced within three hours of the first campaign sign-in in 19 organizations, an authenticator labelled "iPhone 13" activated about 38 minutes after one replay (Huntress timeline, 2026-07-21), and hidden inbox rules with junk names that hid payroll and banking mail. One mailbox was used to send spam to hundreds of outside recipients and another to send a payroll-related email to an outside accountant, which points at business email compromise.

Naming. This campaign has no public name and the operator is unattributed. "Token spraying" is Huntress's name for the technique, not for a campaign or an actor, and this pack uses no other name. The Hacker News headline says 97 compromised accounts; that figure does not appear in Huntress's own text, so it is carried here as an unverified secondary number and not used in any query.

⚠ Defender priority: a password reset does NOT end this compromise. The attacker keeps (1) a live token until sessions are revoked, (2) any authenticator they registered, (3) any rogue Entra device, and (4) hidden inbox rules. Run Q5, Q6 and Q7-Q9 on every account that shows a sign-in from the campaign range, and revoke sessions before resetting. This is an identity and cloud-session attack: the evidence is in Entra sign-in and audit logs and Exchange audit logs, not in endpoint process telemetry.

Hunt Brief & Attack Chain

Working hypothesis: an operator with no public name captures post-MFA sessions through AiTM relays (plus device-code phishing and social engineering), then replays the tokens within minutes from rented addresses in 193.26.115.0/24 using one of two toolsets. Once a session is accepted the operator converts it into durable access: it wipes and re-registers MFA, registers fake Entra devices that mint tokens independently of the stolen session, and plants hidden inbox rules from different rented or VPN addresses to support payroll-themed business email compromise. Replay addresses rotate across four phases, so durable detection sits on the persistence artifacts and the sign-in fingerprints, with the address ranges as enrichment.

H4 Conditional Access blocks (53003) and revoked-session errors (50133) mark valid credentials even when the attacker is stopped. H5 endpoint telemetry sees only the victim reaching the relay. H6 payroll-themed rule criteria identify the BEC objective.

Hardening: Tiered & Deployable

Blocking the replay range does not fix this. The operator rotated across six addresses in four phases, used addresses outside the /24, and captures sessions at relays on other providers. The durable fix makes a stolen post-MFA session less useful and makes the persistence artifacts hard to plant. Authority: MITRE M-numbers plus Microsoft Learn (snapshots 07 to 09) and Huntress; items without a snapshot citation are marked best-practice.

Immediate (this week)
  • Conditional Access: require MFA (or block) for All Users, All Cloud Apps and All Client App types, unconditionally. M1032 / M1036. Huntress guidance; closes the app, group and location scoping gaps.
  • Block or tightly scope the device-code flow with a Conditional Access authentication-flows policy. M1032 / M1036. Huntress and Microsoft Learn both say to restrict it; Microsoft says to get as close to a unilateral block as possible. Start report-only. Playbook 1.
  • Enable mailbox auditing and export all four Entra/Exchange streams (interactive and non-interactive sign-ins, audit, Unified Audit Log) for 90+ days. M1047. Without them Q1-Q12 are blind. Best-practice, no formal benchmark.
  • Run the account containment sequence on every user with a campaign-range sign-in: revoke sessions, then reset, then remove attacker MFA methods, devices and rules. M1018. Huntress and Microsoft Learn. Playbook 3.
Near term (1 to 4 weeks, pilot first)
  • Token Protection Conditional Access policy for Exchange Online, SharePoint Online and Teams on supported Windows, macOS and iOS native clients. M1032. Microsoft Learn: binds sign-in sessions to the device. Limit: browser-based support is narrow, so it does not stop Build B browser replay. Report-only first. Playbook 5.
  • Require a compliant or hybrid-joined device for Microsoft 365 access. M1032 / M1036. A rogue Entra-joined device is not Intune-compliant, and a stolen token replayed from a rented host is not on a managed device. Best-practice; CIS Microsoft 365 Foundations / CIS Entra ID Benchmark cover the family. Pilot, because it affects BYOD.
  • Require MFA to register security information and to join or register devices; restrict who may join devices. M1032 / M1018. Targets the MFA-replacement and rogue-device persistence steps. Best-practice; Microsoft Entra device settings and the Register security information user action.
  • Entra ID Protection risk-based Conditional Access (sign-in risk and user risk). M1036. Entra ID Protection rated the spam mailbox high risk and flagged four replay addresses, per Huntress.
  • Alert on hidden-folder and junk-name inbox rules and on MFA-wipe-and-replace (Q6-Q9). M1047.
  • Disable standard-user consent to new applications. M1018. Huntress advises it; no consent abuse was reported in this campaign. Playbook 6.
Strategic (1 to 3 months)
  • Phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2 or passkeys) through Conditional Access authentication strengths for all users, starting with finance, payroll and admins. M1032. An AiTM relay cannot replay a FIDO2 assertion bound to the real origin. Best-practice.
  • Move to Continuous Access Evaluation and shorter-lived sessions for high-value roles. M1036. Microsoft Learn notes access tokens last one hour by default and that CAE lets admins revoke CAE-capable sessions faster.
  • Outbound spam and external-forwarding limits in Exchange Online. M1021 / M1047. One mailbox sent spam to hundreds of outside recipients before it was flagged. Best-practice.
  • Standing credential-validity triage, not spray-volume triage. M1047. Huntress: ranking tenants by failed attempts points responders at the four organizations where Conditional Access already worked.

3 · Contain one suspect account (revoke, reset, remove attacker methods and devices)
