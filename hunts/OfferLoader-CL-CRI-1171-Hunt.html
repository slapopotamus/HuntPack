01

Executive Summary

CL-CRI-1171 is a pay-per-install (PPI) cybercrime distribution network that Unit 42 (Palo Alto Networks) has tracked for at least two years. The operator sells infection access to independent buyers, each of whom deploys their own payload through the same disposable, generic Inno Setup loader, which Unit 42 named OfferLoader after the offer_execution / offer_execution_fail / offer_exists variable names found in its C2 traffic. Victims are funneled in through two channels: SEO-poisoned search results promoting trojanized versions of legitimate utilities (WinDirStat, Bluetooth drivers), and at least 11 YouTube gaming-optimization channels with hundreds of thousands of subscribers. Unit 42 has identified more than 10,000 distinct loader samples and over 200 rotating two-word-compound C2 domains across the .xyz, .cfd, .space, and .info TLDs.

Once installed, OfferLoader beacons for an install verdict and then spawns three child processes, eld0.exe, eld1.exe, and eld2.exe, each an independent malware campaign with its own C2 and objective. The April 2026 intrusion set Unit 42 documented in depth delivered three payloads: Insomnia RAT (a dual Node.js/Python cross-platform backdoor, user-agent insomnia/2023.4.0 Windows), ARKTunnel (a previously undocumented WebSocket tunneling RAT extracted from a bitmap image via LSB steganography), and Docro Hijacker (a Chrome browser hijacker that forges valid HMAC signatures to bypass Chrome's Secure Preferences integrity check and sideload a search-hijacking extension). A separate June 2026 intrusion delivered two different payloads (GCleaner, Socks5Systemz) through the same loader, confirming the payload slots are interchangeable and not fixed to this campaign's three headline families.

Defender priority: the distinctive, low-noise signal in this pack is the eld0.exe/eld1.exe/eld2.exe child-process fan-out and the exact hashes of the documented samples (Q1, Q3). The rotating two-word-compound domain infrastructure (>200 hostnames observed, expected to keep rotating) is inherently high-false-positive as a standalone signal and is shipped as a low-priority triage feed only (Q10), never a block list. The in-memory BMP steganographic extraction and the in-browser SERP script injection are both invisible to endpoint telemetry by design; see Section 15 for the honest coverage picture.

Hunt Brief & Attack Chain

Hunt hypotheses

  • H1 (high confidence). Any host spawning processes named eld0.exe, eld1.exe, or eld2.exe has run the OfferLoader chain; the naming is unique to this loader family. MITRE T1204.002/T1027. Telemetry: ProcessRollup2. FP: low. → Q1.
  • H2 (medium confidence). A trojanized installer masquerading as windirstat.exe or a Bluetooth-driver installer, executing from a Downloads/Temp path, precedes the eld0-2 fan-out. MITRE T1204.002/T1036.005. Telemetry: ProcessRollup2. FP: medium (legitimate installers also run from these paths). → Q2.
  • H3 (high confidence). Exact-hash matches against the 16 published samples confirm this specific, already-analyzed campaign wave. MITRE T1204.002. Telemetry: ProcessRollup2 (SHA256HashData). FP: low. → Q3.
  • H4 (high confidence). Insomnia RAT's installer disables Defender and excludes the entire C:\ drive before deploying its Node.js/Python agents — a distinctive, rarely-legitimate command-line pattern. MITRE T1562.001. Telemetry: ProcessRollup2 (CommandLine). FP: medium. → Q4.
  • H5 (high confidence). Insomnia RAT persists via two masquerading scheduled tasks with non-Microsoft naming under Microsoft-owned task paths. MITRE T1053.005. Telemetry: ScheduledTaskRegistered. FP: low. → Q5.
  • H6 (medium confidence). Node.js is hidden from Add/Remove Programs via a SystemComponent=1 registry write, correlating with the Insomnia install chain. MITRE T1112. Telemetry: RegGenericValueUpdate. FP: medium. → Q6.
  • H7 (high confidence). ARKTunnel's dropped ZIP (procorTrex.zip), executable (wscl.exe), and Windows service (wscl-13/msvcsrvc) are all distinctively named and not used by legitimate software. MITRE T1027.003/T1543.003. Telemetry: ZipFileWritten, NewExecutableWritten, CreateService. FP: low. → Q7.
  • H8 (medium confidence). Docro Hijacker's Chrome Secure Preferences tamper and docro extension drop under C:\ProgramData\DocsHelper\ are visible as file-write events even though the HMAC-bypass logic itself is not. MITRE T1176/T1112. Telemetry: CriticalFileModified, NewExecutableWritten. FP: medium. → Q8.
  • H9 (high confidence). DNS resolution to any of the 10 confirmed second-stage/C2 domains is a strong, campaign-specific signal, including the crowdstri.com CrowdStrike typosquat. MITRE T1071.001/T1583.001. Telemetry: DnsRequest. FP: low. → Q9.
  • H10 (low confidence, explicit triage-only). The two-word-compound domain naming shape (six-to-eighteen lowercase letters, no digits or hyphens, over .xyz/.cfd/.space/.info) can be pattern-matched, but the shape alone is common to a lot of benign and parked domains. MITRE T1583.001. Telemetry: DnsRequest. FP: high. → Q10.
  • H11 (medium confidence). Insomnia RAT's C2 traffic carries the literal HTTP User-Agent insomnia/2023.4.0 Windows, visible only in ingested web-proxy/NGSIEM logs, not native EDR telemetry. MITRE T1071.001/T1036.005. Telemetry: NGSIEM proxy log fields. FP: low, but shares its UA with the legitimate Insomnia REST client. → Q11.

Two attack-chain steps have no mappable Falcon telemetry and are carried forward as explicit gaps rather than silently dropped: the in-memory LSB steganographic extraction of ARKTunnel's payload from a BMP resource, and the in-browser SERP script injection Docro Hijacker uses to monetize search results. Both are addressed in Section 15.

Native Audit-Log Hunts

These checks require no CQL and directly cover the extension-governance and dropped-artifact gaps noted in Section 15.

RTR one-time fleet sweep (read-only, point-in-time)

Run via Falcon Real Time Response across the fleet to confirm on-disk/service/task presence. See the Fleet Audit Commands quick-copy block in Section 10 for the exact commands (ARKTunnel service check, scheduled-task check, docro extension path check, Secure Preferences modification time check).

Chrome / Edge (Chromium) — managed browser extension inventory

  • Google Admin console → Chrome browser cloud management → Apps & extensions → Users & Browsers report — the sideloaded docro extension has no fixed Chrome Web Store ID (it is sideloaded from disk, not installed from the store), so hunt by installed-extension name/path rather than a store ID.
  • Microsoft Intune / Edge Business inventory — same approach if Edge extension reporting is configured.

Windows Service Control Manager

  • sc query wscl-13 / sc query msvcsrvc across the fleet (or the RTR batch equivalent in Section 10) to find ARKTunnel's installed service before or after a Q7 network hit.

Task Scheduler operational log

  • Enable Microsoft-Windows-TaskScheduler/Operational (disabled by default on most builds) and alert on new task registrations under \Microsoft\Windows\Maps\ or \Microsoft\Windows\Servicing\ that do not match your organization's known-good task baseline — see Playbook 4 in Section 13.

Perimeter DNS / secure web gateway logs

Query Umbrella / Zscaler / firewall logs for the domains in Section 4. Unlike Falcon, a web gateway typically logs the full request path, which is the only way to confirm the Docro extension's /extensionInstaller/updateChromeExtension and similar config-endpoint paths actually fired.

Hardening — Tiered

Immediate — deploy this week

1. Disable local-admin merge for Defender exclusion lists. Set-MpPreference -DisableLocalAdminMerge $true (equivalently, the "Configure local administrator merge behavior for lists" policy under Administrative Templates > Windows Components > Microsoft Defender Antivirus > Exclusions) prevents a locally-run Add-MpPreference -ExclusionPath "C:\" from ever merging into the effective, managed exclusion list — neutralizing t.ps1's defense-evasion step even if it runs. MITRE M1028 (Operating System Configuration); Microsoft Security Baseline for Windows / Microsoft Defender Antivirus documentation; CIS Microsoft Windows Benchmark (Microsoft Defender Antivirus section).

2. Lock the enterprise-managed Chrome default search provider. Set DefaultSearchProviderEnabled/DefaultSearchProviderName/DefaultSearchProviderSearchURL via Chrome Enterprise policy (Playbook 2, Section 13). A managed default-search policy takes precedence over a user-profile Secure Preferences value, so Docro Hijacker's HMAC-forged rewrite of the default search provider stops working even if the bypass DLL still runs. MITRE M1042 (Disable or Remove Feature or Program); CIS Google Chrome Benchmark (Default Search Provider section).

3. Fleet-wide audit sweep. Run the Section 10 Fleet Audit Commands across all endpoints to find existing ARKTunnel services, Insomnia scheduled tasks, and Docro extension/Secure-Preferences artifacts before any policy rollout, so existing infections are triaged rather than silently masked by new controls.

Near term — 1 to 4 weeks

4. Application control for user-writable execution paths. Deploy an AppLocker or WDAC path-deny rule for unsigned executables launched from %USERPROFILE%\Downloads\* and %USERPROFILE%\AppData\Local\Temp\*, with a signed-publisher exception for approved deployment tooling (Playbook 3, Section 13). This blocks the OfferLoader installer's very first execution step regardless of which lure filename it uses next. MITRE M1038 (Execution Prevention); CIS Microsoft Windows Benchmark (Application Control section).

5. Task Scheduler operational logging and baseline alerting. Enable Microsoft-Windows-TaskScheduler/Operational fleet-wide (Playbook 4, Section 13) and alert on new task registrations under \Microsoft\Windows\Maps\ and \Microsoft\Windows\Servicing\ that fall outside your known-Microsoft-task baseline. MITRE M1047 (Audit).

6. DNS-security category blocking for newly-registered domains in the observed TLD set. Most enterprise DNS-security/SWG products offer a "newly registered domain" or "high-risk TLD" category; enabling it for .xyz/.cfd/.space/.info reduces exposure to the rotating gate infrastructure without needing a static, perishable domain list. MITRE M1031 (Network Intrusion Prevention). ⚠ best-practice, no single CIS Benchmark section covers TLD-category DNS filtering; the underlying control (DNS security filtering) is documented in CIS Controls v8, Control 9.

Strategic — 1 to 3 months

7. Browser extension governance program. Move from ad hoc extension checks to a documented allowlist model: ExtensionInstallBlocklist: ["*"] paired with ExtensionInstallAllowlist for approved extensions (Playbook 2, Section 13). This blocks sideloaded/unpacked extensions like docro at the platform level, independent of Docro Hijacker's specific HMAC-bypass technique, and generalizes to the next browser-hijacker family. MITRE M1042; CIS Google Chrome Benchmark (Extension Management section).

8. Formal PPI/loader-cluster IR runbook. Generalize the Section 14 containment runbook into a standing playbook for "generic loader spawns N unrelated payloads" incidents — the defining trait of PPI infrastructure is that any two infections from the same loader can carry entirely different, unrelated malware, so IR scoping must never assume the payload set from one infection generalizes to the next. MITRE M1047 (Audit) as an ongoing IR-maturity practice.

9. User awareness training on SEO-poisoned downloads and gaming-optimization lures. The initial-access vector here requires no exploit, only a convincing download page or YouTube description link; awareness training measurably reduces the population that reaches step 1 of the attack chain (Section 3). MITRE M1017 (User Training).
