Executive Summary

PoeLLM is a Linux ELF botnet implant tracked by Lumen Black Lotus Labs as the Canto Incognito campaign. It has been active since at least April 2026 (first poem commit 2026-04-13) and has impacted 3,400+ victim servers, with a mid-June peak of nearly 2,200 affected servers and roughly 800 active per day. Victims are mostly in the United States and Western Europe. Lumen assesses with moderate confidence that an Italian-speaking, financially motivated actor is behind it.

Entry and targets: the actor scans for and exploits internet-facing open-source and AI services: LiteLLM (default port 4000), Ollama, Gotenberg (port 3000) and Gitea, with possible targeting of Ivanti Sentry. The LiteLLM path is a crafted POST to /mcp-rest/test/connection (command injection, CVE-2026-42271) that tells the target to download the payload from port 81 on the C2. Lumen first found the infrastructure while investigating Ivanti Sentry CVE-2026-10520.

Tradecraft: the payload is an ELF named libgcrypt with a remote shell, XMRig and Iron miners, HTTP/S scanning and exploit delivery. Victims mine to Kryptex endpoints and are reused as scanners and exploit servers, which is how the botnet grows. Its signature trick is the C2 dead drop: the current C2 IPv4 address is derived from four words in a poem titled "On the Nature of Connection" in dash.css of a GitHub repo owned by ejejejdfbbebe (a fork of the nodejs.org source). Changing four words rotates every victim to a new C2, so IP blocklists age quickly. Many C2s were compromised routers running Boa web servers with vulnerable admin pages (CVE-2018-21027 and CVE-2018-21028 are named for the first C2 router; Lumen saw no direct evidence they were exploited by the actor).

Defender priority: this pack is built for the Falcon Linux sensor. First find every internet-reachable LiteLLM, Gotenberg, Gitea, Ollama and Ivanti Sentry instance and patch or hide it (Q10, section 12). Then hunt the host-side signals that survive C2 rotation: a service process spawning a shell or downloader (Q5), a fetch from port 81 (Q6), a server talking to a mining pool (Q3, Q9), and a server fanning out to many public hosts on 3000, 4000 or 22 (Q8). Treat the IP list (Q1) as a retro-hunt, not the defence.

Web-hunter notes and decisions

  • Independence: both news articles derive from the Lumen report and add no atomic indicators of their own. Every atomic IOC is therefore single-sourced, so every indicator in this pack ships detect. None is eligible for prevent or critical.
  • Raw text, not summaries: the three pages were fetched and their plain text saved verbatim as source snapshots (PoeLLM-CantoIncognito-Hunt-sources/); the provenance gate reads those files.
  • Corrections to the request brief, after checking the primary source: (1) port 81 is the payload download port, not a beacon port; the beacon ports are 3778, 5001, 5002 and 9999. (2) CVE-2018-21027 and CVE-2018-21028 are flaws in the router admin pages of C2 hosts; Lumen reports no direct evidence the actor exploited them, so they are not listed as PoeLLM exploits. (3) Ivanti Sentry is "possible targeting" in Lumen's words, tied to CVE-2026-10520 through one compromised victim. (4) Lumen's own text gives both "more than 3,400 victim servers" and "almost 2,200" for the mid-June peak; both figures are reported as stated.
  • Not in the sources: no persistence mechanism, no Iron miner binary name or hash, no fixed-version numbers for CVE-2026-42271 or CVE-2026-10520, and no Ollama CVE. These are flagged as gaps rather than filled in.
  • Prompt-injection check: no embedded instructions or requests directed at an AI agent were found in any fetched page. No source was disqualified.
  • Actor admin host: Lumen assesses, with moderate confidence, that an Italy-hosted server running Prometheus and Uptime Kuma is the operator's management interface. No victim contacts it, so it has no endpoint-detection value and its address is deliberately not shipped as an indicator.
  • Dedupe: name and IOC checks against the published library were clear. The published LiteLLM and Ivanti Sentry packs cover the CVEs; this pack covers the PoeLLM campaign and shares none of their indicators.
