Executive Summary

Attacker objective. SafePay is a closed ransomware operation. It steals data, encrypts systems, and demands cryptocurrency, typically one to three percent of the victim's yearly revenue. [#5] [#6] A joint advisory from the FBI, CISA, the US Secret Service and German and UK police says it has hit at least 600 victims since August 2024 and was among the most active ransomware groups of 2026. [#1]

How it gets in. The advisory lists stolen or guessed passwords, brute force, VPN access from Mullvad addresses, and five exploited edge and backup vulnerabilities: two in Cisco ASA and FTD, two in Veeam Backup & Replication, and one in SonicWall SonicOS. CISA's catalog marks all five as used in ransomware campaigns. [#1] [#2] NCC Group found a firewall policy that let local accounts skip VPN MFA. [#4] Blackpoint reports email flooding followed by Microsoft Teams contact from a fake help desk. [#6]

What it does next. Operators work by hand with built-in tools. They enumerate shares with ShareFinder and SharpShares, dump credentials, install ScreenConnect or AnyDesk, and drop a QDoor backdoor that hollows a WerFault.exe process. [#1] [#4] They stage data with WinRAR, then move it with Rclone, WinSCP, FileZilla, Backblaze b2 or OneDrive. [#1] [#3] Before encrypting they disable Defender and delete shadow copies and, in some cases, factory-reset network backup storage. [#1] [#3] Access to encryption can take under 24 hours. [#5]

Why this pack. CISA published its advisory on 20 October 2026, after the library's existing SafePay pack. This pack is built on that advisory and adds the five KEV-confirmed CVEs, the QDoor chain, and a warning that four of CISA's nine addresses are shared hosting and must not be blocked.

Defender priority: patch or isolate the five edge and backup CVEs now, make sure every VPN account including local ones requires MFA, and alert on regsvr32 launched with -pass (Q1), regsvr32 spawning WerFault (Q3), and recovery inhibition (Q10). Protect backups so encryption cannot also destroy recovery.

Web Hunter decisions

  • Atomic indicators come from CISA only. Every hash, address and hostname in Section 10 appears verbatim in the saved CISA advisory snapshot. NCC Group's QDoor analysis independently confirms 88.119.167[.]239.
  • Four CISA addresses are hunt-only. 104.26.2[.]223, 140.82.114[.]4, 185.199.108[.]133 and 173.194.194[.]94 are in CISA's table, but, as an analyst note rather than a CISA statement, ranges like these commonly belong to large CDN, code-hosting and search providers. CISA itself warns that addresses may be reassigned and should be verified before blocking. Check WHOIS and ASN first. Blocking them could break unrelated services.
  • Dropped. Third-party victim counts that no primary source repeats. The pack uses CISA's figure of at least 600.
  • SHA-1 hashes stay in comments. Falcon IOC Management takes SHA-256 and MD5 only, so SHA-1 is hunted by Q2.
  • No victim names. Victims named in vendor write-ups are not repeated.
  • Existing library pack. SafePay-Ransomware-Hunt (v0.3, 9 October 2026) already covers encryptor flags, discovery, staging and service stops, and carries SentinelOne tabs. This pack overlaps on tradecraft by design so it stands alone, and differs in being built on AA26-293A and adding QDoor, the KEV-confirmed edge CVEs and the hosting-address caution.

Contradictions and gaps

  • Attribution. Blackpoint says researchers link SafePay to former Conti members. Halcyon lists possible former LockBit or ALPHV links. Bitdefender sees LockBit Black code lineage but a different operating model. None is confirmed.
  • Extortion portal. CISA and Blackpoint give Tor addresses. SOCRadar says a TON-hosted portal. Treat Tor as confirmed and TON as unverified.
  • Hostname spelling. CISA lists WIN-SBOE3CPNAL. Huntress shows WIN-SBOE3CPNALE. The pack hunts both.
  • Observation window. CISA's indicators were seen between November 2024 and August 2026. Older addresses may be reassigned.
  • Huntress snapshot. It is the fetch tool's extraction rather than the raw page. Only indicators CISA also lists are shipped as atomic indicators.

Overall confidence: High. A joint government advisory and three independent vendor incident analyses agree on every central claim.

Native Audit-Log Hunts

These checks need no Falcon query. Run them in the product named in each row.

N1 · VPN and firewall: successful logins without MFA, local accounts

Where: On each VPN gateway (the NCC Group case was a FortiGate policy that let local and LDAP groups skip MFA).

# Review every VPN user group and confirm MFA applies to it
# FortiGate (example):  show user group  /  show vpn ssl settings
# Search VPN authentication logs for logins by LOCAL accounts and for source addresses in Mullvad ranges

A successful VPN login from a local account with no MFA record is the NCC Group entry path. Also check for logins by service and admin accounts.

N2 · Edge-device exploitation: Cisco ASA/FTD and SonicWall

Where: Device consoles and syslog.

# Cisco:     show version | include Software
# SonicWall: System > Status, then compare to the vendor advisory for CVE-2024-40766
# Review authentication failures and administrative logins for bursts from one address

Compare the running version against the vendor fix for CVE-2020-3259, CVE-2023-20269 and CVE-2024-40766. All three are KEV entries with known ransomware use.

N3 · Veeam Backup & Replication: version and exposure

Where: On the Veeam server.

(Get-Item 'C:\Program Files\Veeam\Backup and Replication\Backup\Veeam.Backup.Core.dll').VersionInfo.FileVersion
Get-NetTCPConnection -State Listen | Where-Object { $_.LocalPort -in 9392,9401,6160,6162 } | Select-Object LocalAddress, LocalPort

Confirm the build includes the fixes for CVE-2023-27532 and CVE-2024-40711, and that the server is not reachable from the internet. The ports are common Veeam defaults and the file path is typical; confirm both against Veeam's documentation for your version.

N4 · Active Directory: new or recently changed accounts and admin group changes

Where: Domain controller.

Get-ADUser -Filter 'whenCreated -gt $((Get-Date).AddDays(-14))' -Properties whenCreated | Select-Object SamAccountName, whenCreated
Get-ADGroupMember 'Domain Admins' | Select-Object Name, SamAccountName

CISA says SafePay gains admin rights on existing accounts or creates new ones. Investigate any account created or elevated outside a ticket.

N5 · Email and Teams: flooding followed by external help-desk contact

Where: Mail gateway and Teams audit logs.

# Mail gateway: users who received hundreds of messages within an hour
# Teams audit: first-contact chat or call from an external tenant claiming to be IT support

Blackpoint describes spam flooding followed by a Teams approach that leads the user to install remote-access software.

N6 · Backups: unexpected factory resets, deleted restore points, stopped services

Where: Veeam console, NAS audit log, Windows System event log.

Get-WinEvent -FilterHashtable @{LogName='System'; Id=7036; StartTime=(Get-Date).AddDays(-14)} |
  Where-Object { $_.Message -match 'Volume Shadow Copy|Veeam' -and $_.Message -match 'stopped' } |
  Select-Object TimeCreated, Message

CISA says SafePay halts the Volume Shadow Copy Service and resets network backup storage to factory settings. A reset NAS or deleted restore points days before an incident is a strong signal.

N7 · Logons from attacker device names

Where: Domain controllers and VPN servers.

Get-WinEvent -FilterHashtable @{LogName='Security'; Id=4624} -MaxEvents 50000 |
  Where-Object { $_.Properties[11].Value -match '^(WIN-SBOE3CPNALE?|WIN-3IUUOFVTQAR|WIN-DK18BI83M0S|WIN-I1HRRJHCEIV|vultr-guest)' } |
  Select-Object TimeCreated, @{n='Account';e={$_.Properties[5].Value}}, @{n='Workstation';e={$_.Properties[11].Value}}, @{n='SrcIP';e={$_.Properties[18].Value}}

Run on each domain controller. Any hit is an attacker-controlled device authenticating.

N8 · File servers: encrypted files and the ransom note

Where: File-server audit logs or an EDR file search.

Get-ChildItem -Path \\REPLACE_WITH_SERVER\REPLACE_WITH_SHARE -Recurse -Include readme_safepay.txt,*.safepay -ErrorAction SilentlyContinue | Select-Object FullName, LastWriteTime

The note readme_safepay.txt and the .safepay extension confirm encryption. Do not rely on this for early warning.

Q1: SafePay encryptor launched

Subject: 🚨 HUNT ALERT 🚨 — SafePay Encryptor Launched via regsvr32 (Possible Ransomware Deployment)

regsvr32 started with the -pass argument and encryptor options. Huntress saw this exact pattern used to run locker.dll from a network share, two days after the attacker first got in. Encryption may already be running, so act before investigating. Check whether Falcon blocked it, and identify which host and share path started it.

Investigation checklist

  1. Blocked or executed? ProcessBlocked means Falcon stopped it; ProcessRollup2 means it ran.
  2. Read CommandLine for -path=. That share or folder is the encryption target.
  3. Isolate the host now if the process ran; do not wait for a full investigation.
  4. Read ParentBaseFileName and UserName to identify the session that launched it.
  5. Find other hosts running regsvr32 with the same arguments in the last hour.
  6. Check Q10 on the same hosts for shadow copy deletion and recovery changes.
  7. Disable the account and block its sessions; check for new admin accounts.
  8. Protect backups: disconnect backup repositories and storage from the network.

Hardening — Tiered & Deployable

Immediate (this week)
  • Patch or isolate the five exploited devices and servers. Cisco ASA/FTD (CVE-2020-3259, CVE-2023-20269), Veeam Backup & Replication (CVE-2023-27532, CVE-2024-40711), SonicWall SonicOS (CVE-2024-40766). All five are KEV entries with known ransomware use. Framework: MITRE M1051; CISA AA26-293A key action and BOD 26-04 patch prioritization; vendor advisories. Verify: Playbook 1.
  • Require MFA on every VPN account, including local accounts. NCC Group's case began with a policy that let local and LDAP groups skip MFA. Framework: MITRE M1032; CISA CPG 3.F; NCC Group (source 4). Verify: Playbook 1 and native hunt N1.
  • Take backups out of reach. Keep Veeam servers and network storage off the internet, with separate credentials, and keep encrypted, immutable and offline copies. SafePay resets backup storage to factory settings. Framework: MITRE M1053, M1030; CISA CPG 3.K, 3.O. Verify: Playbook 1.
  • Lock down admin passwords. At least 15 characters, failed-login lockout, no reuse. Weak admin passwords decided NCC Group's case. Framework: MITRE M1027; NIST SP 800-63B; CISA CPG 3.B, 3.C, 3.E. Verify: Playbook 6.
  • Block the confirmed infrastructure. Import the Section 10 CSV (five addresses, six hashes). Do not block the four shared-hosting addresses. Framework: MITRE M1037, M1038; CISA IOC guidance.
  • Confirm Defender Tamper Protection is on. SafePay disables Defender before moving laterally. Framework: MITRE M1038; Microsoft Defender documentation. Verify: Playbook 4.
Near term (1 to 4 weeks, pilot first)
  • Run three ASR rules in audit, then block. Block credential stealing from LSASS, block process creations from PsExec and WMI commands, and advanced ransomware protection. Framework: MITRE M1040, M1043; Microsoft ASR rule reference; CIS Windows Benchmark Defender section. Verify: Playbook 2.
  • Limit RDP to jump hosts and gateways. Close inbound RDP elsewhere and disable unused ports. Framework: MITRE M1035, M1030; CISA mitigations. Verify: Playbook 3.
  • Inventory and allowlist remote-access tools. Remove unsanctioned ScreenConnect, AnyDesk and TeamViewer, and alert on new instances. Framework: MITRE M1038, M1042; CISA AA26-293A. Verify: Playbook 5.
  • Monitor DCSync and limit replication rights. Alert on directory-replication requests from non-domain-controller accounts. Framework: MITRE M1026; CIS Active Directory guidance. ⚠ best-practice, no formal benchmark for the specific alert.
  • Control external Teams contact and mail flooding. Restrict external chats to approved tenants and alert on message floods. Framework: MITRE M1017, M1021; Blackpoint (source 6). ⚠ best-practice, no formal benchmark.
  • Filter outbound transfers. Block unapproved cloud-storage and file-transfer destinations from servers. Framework: MITRE M1037; CISA AA26-293A.
Strategic (1 to 3 months)
  • Just-in-time administrator access. Disable admin accounts when not in use and grant time-boxed access on request. Framework: MITRE M1026; CISA AA26-293A mitigations.
  • Segment the network. Separate servers, backups and user networks so one RDP session cannot reach everything. Framework: MITRE M1030; CISA CPG 3.I.
  • Apply zero trust to remote access. Authenticate device and user for every VPN and admin session. Framework: MITRE M1032, M1035; CISA AA26-293A key actions.
  • Practice restore. Test full restoration from offline backups at least twice a year. Framework: MITRE M1053; CISA CPG 3.O.
  • Retire the exposed edge. Replace end-of-life appliances and move remote access to a maintained platform. Framework: MITRE M1051; CISA KEV guidance.

Verify after deployment. Over 14 days, confirm Q1, Q3 and Q10 stay silent, that no VPN login succeeds without MFA, that Veeam is not reachable from the internet, and that ASR audit events show no unexplained blocks before moving to block mode.

Playbook 2 · Attack Surface Reduction rules (T1003.001, T1569.002, T1486; M1040, M1043)

Step 2.1 · Enable three ASR rules in audit mode

What it does: Logs what each rule would block without stopping anything. Covers LSASS credential theft, PsExec and WMI process creation, and ransomware behavior.

Prerequisites: Microsoft Defender Antivirus in active mode; Windows 10 1709 or later or Windows Server 2016 or later. The PsExec and WMI rule is not compatible with managing devices through Configuration Manager.

Reboot required: No.

Configuration
