Executive Summary

Objective. StingyBasilisk is an espionage group that Positive Technologies (PT ESC IR) found during an incident response at a government agency, with presence in that network since at least April 2022. The initial access vector was not determined and PT cannot reliably link the activity to any known APT group. Every implant sample was a DLL built to wait and run its payload in process memory, and control runs over Windows named pipes or a direct network connection, which lets the group reach hosts in network-isolated segments through a chain of compromised neighbours.

Toolset. TediousLoader is a network-triggered backdoor loaded into svchost.exe as Local System through a W32Time Time Provider registry entry. It opens a raw socket on every adapter, reads incoming IP packets and activates on a magic signature. SilentCOM is started by COM hijacking of the Network List Manager and Network Configuration objects. CunningDoor is delivered by DLL side-loading of signed SafeNet DLLs and was pushed with Impacket atexec from February 2026. HenMapCaCon.dll is a Go backdoor that serves TLS over a named pipe.

Highest-value defensive angle. Persistence is the stable signal. A new W32Time provider key, a TreatAs value on two specific COM objects and GUID-named scheduled tasks are cheap to detect and independent of any indicator. Atomic indicators are thin: the retrievable sources publish file names, pipe names, registry paths and in-memory constants, but no hashes, IP addresses or domains. Those are held as placeholders until the full PT report is imported.

Two claims reconciled. A report that TediousLoader decrypts AES-256 shellcode is correct per PT (AES-256-CBC). The modified ChaCha20 constant belongs to the second-stage shellcode that SilentCOM receives, not to TediousLoader, and one secondary report wrongly ties it to HenMapCaCon (section 2).

Defender priority: query W32Time TimeProviders and the two hijacked TreatAs keys across 90 days of registry telemetry now (Q1 to Q3), then sweep for the named DLLs (Q5). A hit on any of these on a host that is not a deliberate test is a probable compromise: isolate it, and treat neighbouring hosts reachable over SMB as in scope.

Decisions and conflicts

  • AES-256 versus ChaCha20. Primary: TediousLoader decrypts received code with AES-256 in CBC mode. CunningDoor also uses AES-256-CBC with a static key. SilentCOM's first payload uses AES-192-CBC, and the second-stage shellcode then does Curve25519 key exchange and ChaCha20 with modified constants. Both secondary claims are partly right and attach to different components. This pack states each against its own tool only.
  • ChaCha20 constant owner. Primary places it in the SilentCOM shellcode memory. Brinztech places it on HenMapCaCon. Not carried forward.
  • Disclosure date. The first public statement is dated 29 October 2026 (Gazeta.Ru quoting PT). The technical article appeared on Habr on 12 November and was covered on 13 November. I could not read PT's own press release page to confirm its date.
  • Where the side-load DLLs sit. PT states files were copied over SMB into C:\Windows\Temp and that shellcode and key files are in that folder. The rundll32 command line names the DLL without a path, so it resolves from the working directory. The folder for the DLLs themselves is an inference, not a quoted fact.
  • Near-duplicate names. The hijack proxies are listed as netprofmprox.dll (Habr prose and example), netcfgprox.dll and netprogmprox.dll (Habr, in the SilentCOM section). The first and third differ by one letter; both are shipped as listed.
  • Prevent posture. Nothing in this pack is set to prevent. Every indicator comes from one primary source, and the two corroborating pages derive from it.
  • Attribution. Unattributed. PT reports no reliable link to a known APT group.

Q1 · W32Time Time Provider DllName set to a non-stock DLL (TediousLoader persistence)
CONF HIGHFP LOWCOST LOW

Looks for: A DllName value written under W32Time\TimeProviders\<provider> that points at anything other than the stock time-service DLLs.
